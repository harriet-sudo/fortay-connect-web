# Fortay Connect qualification, commitment and growth playbook

Prepared 7 May 2026. Based on analysis of 19 sales and customer calls between 7 April and 7 May 2026 (Mark, Yahuda, Max, Deon, Jack, Jonathan, Jess as hosts).

This is the operational counterpart to the website strategy doc. Three jobs: identify time wasters fast, accelerate commitment from real buyers, grow live customers once ROI is proven. Numbers and quotes come from the actual call data.

---

## 1. The pattern the data shows

Of the 19 calls, only 5 were genuinely "moving" (Fulcrum, Anthony Gold, Managed247, G.Network, Tesco Finance). Five more were qualified discovery with real intent but no locked next step (GIACOM, Whistl, Footasylum, LCF Law, Gunnercooke). Three were live customer expansion (ECG dental, Civil Rail Solutions, Acushnet). Two were time wasters (Riverside, Wise). One was wrong ICP (St Albans school, public sector). The rest were mid funnel with mixed signals.

The single biggest difference between the moving deals and the stuck ones is whether a specific next meeting was locked in the same call. Fulcrum locked supplier onboarding plus demos. Anthony Gold locked a 11 May demo with the Private Client Heads. Managed247 locked a mid May POC. G.Network locked the £1000 a day five day commitment offer. Tesco Finance locked Mark into the June board proposal materials. Every one of the deals that moved has an end date, a named human, and a calendar slot.

The deals that go quiet do not have those three things. Apply that filter and you have the qualification engine.

## 2. Time waster red flags (six patterns and what to do about them)

These are the exact signals from the calls. Use them as a qualifier within the first 15 minutes of a discovery call. Two or more flags means qualify out or down.

**Flag 1: No show plus rescheduling loop.** Riverside is the textbook case. The contact missed the call, did not respond, Jess was instructed to call back in a week and then again if no response. That is two weeks of pipeline time for a 10 minute conversation. Action: after one no show with no response within 48 hours, do not chase a second time. Send a one line "let me know when timing works" and move on. Mark the deal as cold.

**Flag 2: Far future deadline with no interim milestone.** Wise is the cleanest example. Mitel expires July 2027, the seasonal HEAT team is unclear, and the follow up note says "January 2024" (a date error that signals nobody is paying attention). Action: ask "what decision gate hits in the next 90 days". If there is none, say "let me check back in six months. For now we will pause." Saves three calls.

**Flag 3: Exploration mode with no champion.** GIACOM has investor approval for the UC strategy but Gillian "controls the calendar" and the timeline is "still being developed". Action: before sending materials, ask "who owns the go versus no go decision" and "should I loop them in now or would you prefer to brief them first". If the answer is vague, say "I will send a one page summary and we will reconvene when there is a named decision maker on the call".

**Flag 4: Sector mismatch.** St Albans is a school group at 190 devices and £4k a quarter. Jonathan correctly bowed out and recommended GoToConnect. The lesson is the call should have ended at minute three. Action: in the first five minutes ask "are you private sector, are you over 200 employees and are you UK based". If any answer is no, refer or decline. Build this as the first qualification step in HubSpot.

**Flag 5: Enterprise overshoot.** Wise again. Global fintech with seasonal HEAT teams and a procurement led RFP cycle. Acushnet (Titleist and FootJoy) is similar at the global level, although the local UK D2C team may still be in scope. Action: ask "how many decision makers will sign off this purchase". If the answer is more than four, ask "is procurement leading or is the operational team". If procurement leads, manage timeline expectations and triage the deal as long cycle. Do not assume same urgency as a 500 person consultancy.

**Flag 6: Generic questions with no second meeting.** Footasylum is the warning sign. Strong stated automation goal (30 agents today, target 6 with 80% automation), Natter as the incumbent, but Jennifer left the call with "Natter would need to offer capabilities that couldn't be achieved with current platform" and no demo booked. Action: never end a discovery call without proposing two specific demo dates. Script: "Let me show you how UJet handles your 30 to 6 agent path. Are you free 3pm Thursday or 10am Friday for 30 minutes". If the prospect declines both, say "I will follow up in four weeks. If you want to move sooner, hit reply." Then move on.

The qualification question that catches most of these in one go is: "What is the trigger event that takes you from evaluating to deciding".

If the buyer cannot name an event, a date, or a person, you have a time waster.

## 3. Commitment accelerators (six moves that worked in the data)

These are the moves Fortay actually used in the calls that closed momentum. Standardise them.

**Accelerator 1: The G.Network play. Paid sprint, fee waived on platform selection.** Mark offered G.Network a five day consultancy at £1000 a day (£5000 total) with the cost waived if they pick a Fortay recommended platform. He paired it with the 391% ROI claim on conversational AI. This is the SPIFF flywheel made visible to the buyer: Fortay earns through the platform commission, so the consulting fee is genuinely waivable. Use this whenever a prospect is comparing two or more platforms and stalling. Time bound the offer to 30 days. Make the deliverable concrete (demos, requirements, recommendation letter to the board).

**Accelerator 2: Anchor on an external hard deadline.** Avaya end of life at Atlas (Jude project), Genesis go live on 30 May at Tesco Finance, growth from 70 to 200 users at Fulcrum. Each of these deals has speed because the buyer does not own the deadline, an external event does. Ask in every call: "When does your current system or contract hit a hard limit". If they name a date, say "to hit that we need platform locked by [eight weeks before]. What does your evaluation cycle look like".

**Accelerator 3: Demo or POC booked in the same call.** Fulcrum, Anthony Gold and Managed247 all booked the next step before the call ended. Anthony Gold pulled forward the demo to the 11 May Private Client Heads meeting. Managed247 locked the mid May POC. Fulcrum agreed super user availability for the upcoming demos. The data is clear. Do not leave a discovery call without a calendar slot for the next thing. Script: "Let me put 30 minutes in for a demo. Are you free Thursday at 3pm or Friday at 10am". If they need someone else in the room: "What is their email, I will send the invite to you both".

**Accelerator 4: Surface the internal champion and lock their accountability.** Gunnercooke has Philip ready to brief Alice (innovation manager) on a £7500 a month Moneypenny replacement. Anthony Gold has Helen pulling the demo onto the Private Client Heads agenda. These champions move deals if you give them a clear thing to do and a date. Script: "Who else at your level needs to be part of this. Should I send them an invite to next call or would you prefer to brief them first. When will you update them. Let's schedule the follow up for three days after that". Never let the champion walk out without a date.

**Accelerator 5: Live pain plus allocated budget.** VEM (Vibrant Energy Matters) named the live pain (10,000 monthly appointments, scheduling fail rate, AI agent Rose already deployed at 50% auto booking with 80% target) and confirmed they wanted to review materials and lock a demo. Managed247 named visibility, SLAs, KPIs and agent availability as the gap and Zoom showed exact fit. When pain is live and budget is implicit (not negotiating yet), demos beat pricing conversations. Script: "This costs you money every day unfixed. Let me show you how it closes the gap and we can talk numbers once you see it work."

**Accelerator 6: Internal presentation request as a moving signal.** When a prospect asks for slides, a deck, or anything to "show my team", that is a buying signal. Tesco Finance (Helen) asked Mark for materials and examples for the June board proposal. VEM agreed to review materials before the demo. Script: "I will send the deck. When are you reviewing with your team and should I join the call". If they say no, then "no problem, when you have reviewed let's grab 15 minutes to discuss what came up". This locks the next conversation.

## 4. Two minute qualification card (use on every discovery call)

If Mark or any of the team can answer all five of these inside the first 15 minutes of a call, it is a real deal. Two or more no answers means qualify out.

What is the trigger event that takes this from evaluating to deciding (date, contract end, growth milestone, regulatory deadline).

Who owns the go versus no go decision and is that person joining the next meeting.

What is your budget cycle and is this in the current year capex or opex.

What does success look like 90 days after go live in numbers (call deflection, agent cost, response time, customer satisfaction).

Are you private sector, UK based, between 200 and 5000 employees.

Mark this on the HubSpot deal card. If three or more are unanswered after the first call, the deal sits in nurture, not pipeline. Stops Mark and the team from chasing ghost deals.

## 5. Live customer growth map (where the next pound comes from)

Three of the 19 calls were existing customers with clear expansion paths. Build these into a customer success motion that runs once a quarter for every named customer.

**Soft phone to cloud to AI (Civil Rail Solutions).** Civil Rail were on Yealink soft phones at £12 per user per month and moved to RingCentral at £15 per user per month. The next step is the Pipedrive integration, then sales intelligence at £25 to £45 per user per month. Each step is a separate decision, not a bundle. Use this with every soft phone and unified communications customer.

**Legacy platform replacement plus virtual agents (Atlas / Jude project).** Avaya end of life triggered the cloud move. Once the cloud platform is in, virtual agents land naturally because they solve the same labour cost pain that drove the move in the first place. Use this with every legacy unified communications customer in the next 12 months of contract.

**Phone plus CRM to sales intelligence to revenue accelerator (Fulcrum).** Fulcrum are evaluating Zoom, RingCentral, GoTo and Dialpad with a 70 user Day 1 base growing to 200. Once the platform is live, the upsell path is sales intelligence (WhatsApp, sentiment, deal scoring at £25 to £45 per user) then revenue accelerator. Sequence matters. Lead with base price, then add ons explicitly. Never bundle. Let the customer choose adoption tempo.

**Contact centre to virtual agent and AI transcription (Anthony Gold).** Moneypenny costs £7500 a month for intake. AI virtual agent at roughly 80p per minute plus a smaller human team comes in around £1500 a month, an 80% reduction. The same customer then upgrades into AI transcription for billable hours, which is incremental revenue rather than cost reduction. Use this with every legal customer.

**Evaluation to POC to implementation (Managed247).** When a customer commits to a POC the deal is mentally 80% done. Lock POC success criteria upfront in writing. "If we hit X, Y and Z over a four week trial, you sign". This stops POC drift and forces a decision at the end of the trial.

For every live customer, the quarterly question to Lynsey (CSM) is: which of the five paths above are they on, what is their next milestone, and what is the value of the next step.
