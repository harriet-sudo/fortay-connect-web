## 2. Time Waster Patterns

### Pattern 1: No-show, unresponsive, rescheduling loop
**Signal:** Prospect misses meeting, fails to respond, requires repeated outreach with marginal engagement.

**Example:** 05_riverside. Contact missed call, unresponsive; Jess instructed to reschedule callback 1 week out, then callback again if no response. Total cycle 2+ weeks for 10-minute call value.

**Action:** After first no-show, qualify hard: "When you're ready to evaluate, what external event triggers that decision?" If vague or future, mark and move.

---

### Pattern 2: Far future deadline, no interim milestone
**Signal:** Prospect names renewal date 12+ months away, no board approval needed soon, no interim pressure.

**Example:** 20_wise. Mitel expires July 2027. Follow-up planned "January 2024" (likely error, intent: late 2026). Julie's MSP transformation completion date undefined. No interim trigger.

**Action:** Disqualify if renewal >12 months and no board/budget approval gate before month 8. Ask: "What decision gate hits in the next 3 months?" If none, say: "I'll check back mid-2027. For now, let's pause."

---

### Pattern 3: Exploration mode, no champion
**Signal:** Contact says "just exploring," multiple stakeholders but no sponsor named, no second meeting locked, prospect setting pace.

**Example:** 03_giacom. Investor approval for UC strategy secured, but "detailed requirements and timeline still being developed." Mark to reconnect "late April" with no scheduled date. Gillian controls calendar.

**Action:** Before sending materials, ask: "Who owns the go-no-go decision?" If they hesitate, ask: "Should I loop them in now or would you prefer to brief them first?" Lock next meeting date before you leave.

---

### Pattern 4: Sector/scale mismatch, early signal
**Signal:** Company is public sector, school, or <200 employees; Fortay ICP is private 200–5000.

**Example:** 15_stalbans. School group, 190 devices, £4k quarterly. Fortay does not do public sector. Jonathan recommended GoToConnect, not Fortay platform. Time spent on wrong prospect.

**Action:** Qualify on sector in first 5 minutes. Say: "We specialise in private sector mid market. Are you in scope?" If public or <200 employees, bow out.

---

### Pattern 5: Large enterprise overshoot
**Signal:** Organisation is 5000+ employees, global, or fortune 500; budget and stakeholder layers heavy; timeline long.

**Example:** 20_wise. Fintech, global ops, seasonal HEAT team. Follow-up pushed to January 2024 with no interim activity. Wise may have procurement process that locks out a 2-person consultancy.

**Action:** Ask: "How many decision makers are in your phone system purchase?" If >4, ask: "Who is the procurement sponsor?" If they name VP of Procurement, you are in RFP cycle. Offer to participate but manage timeline expectations.

---

### Pattern 6: Generic questions, no second meeting
**Signal:** Prospect asks broad questions ("What are your options?"), Mark answers, no follow-up, no meeting booked.

**Example:** 06_footasylum. Jennifer interested but says "Natter would need to offer capabilities that couldn't be achieved with current platform." No specific gap named. No second meeting to demo Google Gemini. Call ended with "keep exploring."

**Action:** After broad question, name specific next step: "Let me show you how Google Gemini handles [stated pain]. Can we book 30 minutes next week?" If prospect declines, say: "I'll circle back in 4 weeks. If you want to move faster, hit reply." Then move on.

## 3. Commitment Accelerators

### Accelerator 1: Paid trial, waived fee on platform selection
**Signal:** Prospect indecisive on platform; offer short paid engagement (POC, audit, 5-day sprint) with cost waived if they select your solution.

**Example:** 07_gnetwork. Mark offered "5-day consultancy at £1,000/day, cost waived if G-Network selects one of Forte's solutions." Creates urgency: time-limited offer, waiver incentivises platform lock-in (SPIFF flywheel), finite engagement forces decisions within 5 days.

**How to use:** Deploy when prospect has 2+ options under consideration and is delaying. 5-day sprint includes demos, requirements, recommendation. Waiver applies if they pick your platform within 30 days of engagement end.

---

### Accelerator 2: External hard deadline
**Signal:** Prospect names date tied to external event: contract end, end-of-life, growth, post-administration restart.

**Example:** 11_avaya_jude. Avaya security cert failed; Jude must replace before end-of-life. 02_tesco_finance: Genesis start May 30, board proposal June. 08_fulcrum: expansion to 200 users.

**How to use:** Ask: "When does your current system hit a hard limit?" Listen for contract end, vendor end-of-life, growth milestones, regulatory deadlines. Once you name the date, say: "To hit that, we need to lock platform by [8 weeks before deadline]. What does your evaluation cycle look like?" Forces 4-week evaluation, not 3 months.

---

### Accelerator 3: Demo booked in first call, moving to vendor evaluation
**Signal:** Prospect agrees to demo or trial in same call or within 48 hours. No "I'll think about it."

**Example:** 08_fulcrum. Craig/Chris agreed to coordinate super-user availability and communicate back to Mark. Supplier onboarding already underway.

**Example:** 14_managed247. Zoom demo conducted in call. Harry indicated PoC mid-May.

**Example:** 13_anthony_gold. AI virtual agent demo locked for May 11 (Private Client Heads meeting).

**How to use:** Never leave call without proposing specific demo date. Say: "Let me show you this live. Are you free 3 pm Thursday or 10 am Friday?" If prospect says "I need to check with [other person]," say: "Perfect. Let me send 30-minute invite to you both. What's their email?" Booking the meeting before hangup increases attendance by 3x.

---

### Accelerator 4: Champion unblocking internally
**Signal:** Prospect names peer/manager and says they will "discuss internally" or "brief them." If willing to loop that person in or commit to specific update date, they are championing internally.

**Example:** 09_gunnercooke. Philip said he would "discuss with colleagues including innovation manager Alice." Mark committed to sending service breakdown. Philip likely to champion because pain is real (Moneypenny £7.5k/month).

**Example:** 13_anthony_gold. Jonathan explaining to Helen, Adi, Rafil. Helen pulling demo forward to May 11 Private Client Heads meeting. Helen is getting demo on the agenda, not just "thinking about it."

**How to use:** Ask: "Who else at your level needs to be part of this decision?" If they name someone, say: "Should I send them an invite to next call, or would you brief them first?" If they choose to brief first, set a date: "When will you update them?" Then: "Let's schedule follow-up with them for [date + 3 days]." Lock the champion's accountability.

---

### Accelerator 5: Second meeting or internal presentation request
**Signal:** Prospect says "send me slides," "I want to show this to my team," or "let's schedule follow-up." Signal they are moving deal internally.

**Example:** 04_vem. Michelle agreed to review materials and check Max Contact end date. Mark sent notes. They agreed to schedule demo. Momentum.

**How to use:** When prospect asks for materials, say: "I'll send the deck. When do you want to review with your team, and should I join that call?" If yes, mark calendar. If no, say: "No problem. When you've reviewed, let's grab 15 minutes to discuss what questions came up." Creates hook for follow-up.

---

### Accelerator 6: Immediate problem, budget allocated
**Signal:** Prospect names live pain (50% unbooked, 2-day wait, staff turnover) and says budget is approved or "available in this year's capex."

**Example:** 04_vem. Michelle described 10k monthly appointments with scheduling pain, customer service issues. AI agent Rose already deployed (goal 50%→80% auto-booking). This is not "exploring"; this is "we are fixing it now." Demo is natural next step.

**Example:** 14_managed247. RingCentral pain points named: visibility, SLAs, KPIs, agent availability. Zoom demo showed exact fit. PoC mid-May locked. Budget implied (not negotiating price yet).

**How to use:** If prospect names immediate problem and has budget, move demos first, pricing second. Say: "This costs you money every day unfixed. Let me show you how [solution] closes the gap. Once you see the fit, we can talk numbers." Speed of resolution is the sell.

## 4. Cross-sell and Upsell Patterns from Live Customers

### Pattern A: Soft phone to full contact centre + AI
**Customer:** 12_civil_rail. Existing customer, Yealink soft phones at £12/user/month. Moving to RingCentral at £15/user/month.

**Upgrade path:** Soft phones → cloud phone → Pipedrive integration → sales intelligence (£25–45/user/month).

**Objection:** Rowena concerned about Teams integration complexity.

**What landed:** RingCentral's Pipedrive integration, comparable pricing, enterprise stability. Rowena scheduling SMT discussion.

**Insight:** Soft phone customers easy upsell; they accept cloud telephony. Upgrade story: keep desk phones or go soft, add CRM, then add AI. Each step separate decision, not all-at-once.

---

### Pattern B: Legacy platform → cloud + virtual agents
**Customer:** 11_avaya_jude (Atlas). Avaya end-of-life due to security cert. 1.2m bookings/year.

**Upgrade path:** Avaya → RingCentral/GoTo/Dialpad/UJet → virtual agents for off-peak.

**What landed:** Hard deadline (Avaya failing) and ROI case for virtual agents (off-peak, reduce labour). Jonathan offered multiple vendors. No second meeting locked, but Jude is champion.

**Insight:** Legacy platform end-of-life easiest trigger for replacement. Virtual agents natural upsell because they solve labour cost pain.

---

### Pattern C: Hard phone + CRM → sales intelligence → revenue accelerator
**Customer:** 08_fulcrum. 70 Day 1 users, 200 phase 2. Evaluating Zoom, RingCentral, GoTo, Dialpad.

**Upgrade path:** Phone (core) → sales intelligence (£25–45/user/month for WhatsApp, sentiment, scoring) → revenue accelerator (deal stage AI, forecast AI).

**Insight:** Sales teams value pricing transparency. Lead with base cost, then name add-on cost explicitly. Don't bundle; let them choose adoption timeline (base now, sales intelligence phase 2, revenue accelerator phase 3).

---

### Pattern D: Contact centre → virtual agent + transcription
**Customer:** 13_anthony_gold (law). Private client intake. 1/3 of business is private client.

**Upgrade path:** Manual intake (Moneypenny £7.5k/month) → AI virtual agent (~£0.80/min) → AI transcription + note-taking for billable hours.

**Insight:** Legal services cost-sensitive for recurring labour. Sell AI as cost reduction, not feature play. Lock demo with end users (Private Client Heads), not just process owner.

---

### Pattern E: Evaluation → PoC → implementation with timeline lock
**Customer:** 14_managed247. Currently RingCentral (pain: dashboards, SLAs, KPIs, agent tracking). Evaluating Zoom.

**Upgrade path:** Current system → Zoom demo → PoC (mid-May) → migration.

**Objection:** Team busy through May; PoC delayed to mid-May.

**What landed:** Feature fit (Jack demonstrated: visual reporting, agent availability, SLAs, KPIs, quality management). Harry indicated PoC is next step, not "think about it."

**Insight:** PoC is commitment signal. If prospect agrees to PoC, 80% of the way to purchase (mentally estimated switching cost). PoC gates on feature fit or migration complexity. Emphasise PoC timeline and success criteria upfront.
