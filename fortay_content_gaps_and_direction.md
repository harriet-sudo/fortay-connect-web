# Fortay Connect website content gaps and direction

Prepared 7 May 2026 by Harriet's Cowork session.

Source material: 15 Mark Taylor sales calls (Nov 2025 to May 2026), the live website source in this repo, the anti AI writing rules PDF, and the founder brief Harriet supplied.

This document has three jobs. Tell you what the calls and the site together actually show. Diagnose why prospects keep disappearing post proposal. Give you a sharp content plan with what to write, in what order, and what is still missing before we start drafting.

---

## 1. The single biggest finding

Across the 15 named sales calls, the AI proposition is winning attention and losing commitment.

Discovery calls go well. Mark is articulate on multi platform expertise. Prospects lean in when shown quantified ROI, the Revenue Accelerator dialer, or a virtual agent setting up in seven minutes. The problem is what happens after.

Three patterns repeat across the deals that stalled (Screwfix, the wealth manager, the Mitel manufacturer, Covermore):

The buyer is not afraid of the technology. The buyer is afraid of the change. Multi stakeholder politics, eleven month evaluation cycles, "two weeks of work into a day" workload stress, and adoption failure on already deployed kit (the £25m construction company bought Zoom phone and rejected it) all point the same way. Implementation risk is the real blocker, not product choice.

The AI proposition is strong on use case but thin on vertical proof. Prospects in legal, manufacturing, financial services and travel insurance keep asking the same underlying question: has this worked for someone like me. The case study bank does not yet answer that.

Quantified savings alone do not close. Screwfix saw £25,000 a month in NICE replacement and still has not advanced. The wealth manager saw NICE drop from £75k to £55k and is still 3 to 4 months behind schedule. Cost is permission to talk, not a reason to sign.

If this is right, the website's job is not to sell the AI. The website's job is to remove implementation risk, prove vertical capability, and qualify in the buyers who can actually move.

## 2. Real prospect language to use on the site

Pulled verbatim from the call summaries. These are the phrases buyers reached for when describing their problem. Treat this as gold and mirror it directly in copy. None of these sound like AI marketing.

On platform sprawl: "three platforms doing what one could", "voicemails frequently full and staff not regularly checking them", "complex and bespoke setups", "low Net Promoter Score of 4".

On AI overwhelm: "adding another set of tools alongside Microsoft Teams would confuse employees", "consumption based charges for features like transcripts and summaries", "AI feels like a buzzword, not a business outcome" (already on the site, keep it).

On internal capacity: "varying experiences and stakeholder involvement over the past six months", "two weeks of work into a day", "one telephony engineer managing Genesys who is also handling AI and automation work" (Covermore).

On adoption: "user adoption issues" after a phone upgrade, "basic requests like changing routing and ringtones took an unusually long time to resolve".

What buyers want next: "automatic transcription, bullet point lists, topic summaries, proposal generation" (the law firm), "virtual agents to handle follow up calls and administrative tasks" (FERSA), "seamless agent handoffs and enhanced tracking" (DealerWeb), "virtual agent in seven minutes" (the law firm again, on setup speed).

What scares them: "how do I manage AI agent quality and brand risk", "complexity of integrations with ITSM toolsets", "consumption based pricing for transcripts and summaries".

The smallest and most useful copy unit is one of these phrases plus a Fortay outcome. That structure beats every abstract value proposition currently on the site.

## 3. What the website is doing well

The hero narrative is template quality. CA Auto Finance, three platforms to one, 40% cost reduction, six weeks audit to live, with a real customer name. This is exactly what the writing rules ask for. Replicate this structure across every other industry page.

The five ICP role cards in the hero (COO, CRO, CTO, Head of CX, Transformation Director) speak in the buyer's own words. "CX spend climbing. Conversion plateauing. No clear line from technology to revenue." That is what a CRO actually says. Keep, deepen, and turn each into its own landing page (see section 7).

Three testimonials read as voice of customer not marketing voice. Justine Long at CA Auto Finance ("Mark was completely impartial and focused on what we actually needed, not what was easy to sell"), Ben Hallatt at Ashtons Legal ("we thought we just needed a phone upgrade"), and INEOS ("they didn't just sell us technology, they understood our business first"). Use these as anchors and commission three more in the same shape from manufacturing, retail and travel insurance.

The platform neutrality FAQ is the strongest differentiation statement on the whole site. "No. We're proudly platform neutral. We evaluate all leading UC, CX, and AI platforms on merit and recommend what's right for your organisation, not what earns us the best margin." Move this above the fold somewhere. It directly answers what a buyer wonders when they see Mark talk about Zoom, RingCentral, 8x8, Genesys, Boost AI and Dialpad in the same breath.

The Process section has clear outcomes per stage. The four stages tied to business outcomes ("clear strategic direction before capital is deployed", "sustained ROI and operational clarity long after deployment") work and should stay.

The Glossary, Assessment and ROI Calculator are smart SEO and lead generation moves. They give qualified buyers something to do that is not yet a sales call.

## 4. What the website is doing wrong

Three CTAs compete on the homepage. Book a Strategy Call (HubSpot), Take the CX Assessment (Assessment), and Book Your Free CX Audit (in the CTA section). Cold buyers do not know which one applies to them. Pick one primary CTA per visitor stage and demote the rest.

DifferentiatorsSection duplicates WhyFortaySection. Same six metrics, different visual layout, no incremental copy. Delete it or repurpose it as a "how we prove it" section with three named customer examples per claim.

The mid funnel sections drop into AI sounding language and undo the work the hero does. Specific offenders to rewrite:

"While platforms push monthly AI releases, we're already validating real-world outcomes. You get proven capabilities, not marketing promises." Banned "not X but Y" structure. Comparative shade without specifics. Rewrite to "We test capabilities in your environment for two weeks before we recommend them. Platforms release monthly. We validate quarterly."

"This is strategic oversight, not implementation." Defensive. Rewrite to "Strategic leadership through delivery. Implementation is led with your team and platform engineers, not handed off."

"CX, Communications and AI Transformation, Designed for Measurable Impact." Generic. "Designed for" and "measurable impact" are everywhere. Replace with the specific commercial outcome: "CX, communications and AI transformation that pays back inside one year."

"Validated" as a metric in the differentiators section. Validated against what. Rewrite to "tested across 50 plus deployments" or whatever the real number is.

"No offshore hand-offs" as the UK based proof point. Asymmetric and unclear. Rewrite to "UK based engineering team in your time zone. No 9am handover from a night shift in Manila."

The FAQ defines the ICP as "50 to 5,000+ employees". That is a 1 to 100 range. The brief says 200 to 5000. Tighten this on the site to match the actual ICP and split into two tiers if needed: 200 to 800 and 800 plus.

There is no dedicated managed services or post deployment page even though the 98% retention claim and the 30+ years experience point both rest on it. This is where Fortay's real moat lives (the calls confirmed CSM keeps customers more than feature parity does) and the site is silent on it.

The role cards do not have role specific landing pages. Every persona currently lands on the homepage and reads horizontal copy. A COO at a 1,500 person manufacturer needs different proof than a Head of CX at a travel insurer.

The technology pages are reasonable but the industry pages need depth audit. Worth checking each industry page against the call language to see whether the manufacturer page reflects what manufacturers actually say.

## 5. The disappearance problem and what to do about it

This is the question Harriet asked first. Why do Screwfix and Car Finance 247 go quiet after proposal.

Three contributing causes from the calls:

Internal politics and multi stakeholder fatigue. The wealth manager's eleven months of evaluation, three to four months behind schedule, with CEO and CFO involvement, is the textbook case. Fortay's proposal was probably fine. The buyer cannot get alignment. There is no single thing the website can do about this, but a "buyer's guide" PDF that gives the COO ammunition for the CFO conversation will help. Same for a board ready ROI summary template.

No vertical proof. The five named verticals (finance, legal, professional services, retail, manufacturing) need at least one named case study each on the site. CA Auto Finance covers finance. Ashtons covers legal. INEOS gestures at industrial. There is nothing concrete in retail or in manufacturing for a Mitel buyer.

Implementation risk language is missing. Buyers are afraid of adoption failure. The construction company bought Zoom phone and rejected it. Covermore has one engineer. Manufacturers are juggling Mitel end of life and PSTN at five sites. The site says "audit to live in six weeks" but does not address what happens between week six and month six. Add a "what month two looks like" content block to each industry page. Show an adoption curve, a managed services hand back, an example KPI report at the 90 day mark.

## 6. Use Harriet on the site

Harriet flagged this herself. She is unusual in this market (34, female, opinionated, intelligent, strong presence in the room) and Fortay does not show her. The hero shows logos and stats. The testimonials quote customers about Mark.

There is a real opportunity to put Harriet on screen. Three concrete moves:

A short founder video on the homepage, 60 to 90 seconds, sentence case captions, with Harriet talking through one specific buyer story (the construction company that bought Zoom and rejected it is perfect because it is honest about adoption failure and what Fortay does differently). This is exactly the kind of human credibility the AI sounding mid funnel sections lack.

A "Sharp Take" insights series under Harriet's name. Provocative, specific, technical. Topic seed list from the calls: "Genesys is the wrong tool for 80% of the people who buy it" (Covermore evidence), "Why your £25k a month NICE saving will not actually close" (Screwfix evidence), "The contact centre licensing tier is a 6x multiplier and nobody warns you" (the wealth manager evidence). One a fortnight is enough.

Harriet's bio on the About page should not read like a CV. It should read like the founder note Stripe used to do. One paragraph on what she is trying to fix in the market and why this combination of skills is doing it.

Mark already has the sales front. Harriet on the website opens up the strategic and the technical front, which is also what differentiates Fortay from Acceleraate (services first), Future Voice (outbound machine), and Softcat / CDW / Wavenet (scale plays).

## 7. Recommended content priority list

Ordered by impact on conversion and SEO together. The first four are high impact and low cost. Five through eight are larger investments. Nine and ten are nice to have.

1. Resolve the CTA conflict on the homepage. Pick one primary path (the audit / assessment is lower friction) and demote the others. Test for two weeks. This costs nothing and probably moves the conversion needle the most.

2. Rewrite the ten lines flagged in section 4. Same site, same structure, sharper voice. One afternoon of work. Fixes the AI tell that undermines the hero.

3. Cut DifferentiatorsSection or repurpose it as "How we prove it" with three named customer mentions per claim. Removes a duplicate and adds proof.

4. Tighten the ICP definition in the FAQ from "50 to 5,000+" to the real 200 to 5000 with two tier handling. Sharper qualification, fewer wasted strategy calls.

5. Build five role specific landing pages: /for-coo, /for-cro, /for-cto, /for-head-of-cx, /for-transformation-director. Each one has the role's pain card from the hero, two specific buyer stories from that role, role specific ROI maths, and a single CTA. Use the sales call language verbatim. Drives ad spend and outbound to relevant landing pages instead of the homepage.

6. Build vertical proof depth on the four industry pages. Each one needs at least one named case study, one industry specific use case for AI agents, the language buyers in that vertical use (finance buyers talk about consumption pricing risk; legal buyers talk about NPS and time billing; manufacturers talk about end of life systems and multi site PSTN; retail buyers talk about peak season and adoption). Add travel insurance as a fifth vertical given the Covermore signal.

7. Build the missing managed services and post deployment page. Deep content on "what month two looks like", governance dashboards, the 90 day KPI report, the named customer multi year stories. This is where the 98% retention claim is earned.

8. Launch the Harriet video on the homepage and the first three Sharp Take posts. Founder visibility is currently zero. Pivoting this is low cost and high differentiation.

9. Build a board ready proposal toolkit (one page summary, ROI model, risk register) as a gated download. Helps the buyer past internal politics. Captures email.

10. Schema markup, internal linking audit, FAQ schema on every industry page. SEO hygiene. Worth it but lower impact than the above.

## 8. SEO direction

The Search Console connection has not been pulled into this analysis yet. That is a follow up. From what is visible in the current site metadata and the brief:

Primary clusters to own: AI agents (broken into reception, sales, customer service, after sales / service), conversational intelligence, contact centre consultancy, unified communications transformation, AI advisory.

Long tail opportunities suggested by the calls: "Genesys alternatives for mid market", "RingCentral RingCX migration", "Mitel end of life replacement", "Microsoft Teams contact centre integration". These are real terms real prospects use.

Vertical clusters: each industry page should target two to three vertical specific phrases. Manufacturing: "manufacturing communications transformation", "Mitel migration manufacturing". Legal: "case management telephony integration", "law firm contact centre". Retail: "retail contact centre AI", "peak season call deflection". Finance: "wealth management communications", "FCA compliant contact recording".

Once we have actual Search Console data we can rank these by current position, search volume and intent. That is the first thing on the questionnaire below.
