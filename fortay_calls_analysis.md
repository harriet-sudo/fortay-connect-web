## 7. RECURRING THEMES ACROSS CALLS

1. **Platform consolidation and Microsoft Teams integration:** Every call mentions Teams or Zoom integration complexity; desire for single integrated system recurring across legal, manufacturing, retail verticals.

2. **Legacy system end of life creating urgency:** Avaya, Mitel, Genesys legacy driving buyer timelines. Fortay's role: help navigate migration complexity and vendor selection.

3. **Cost pressure and contact centre licensing barriers:** Contact centre costs 6x higher than back office; prospects want functionality (call recording, quality management) but balk at price. Virtual agents positioned as cost alternative.

4. **AI transcription and quality management as hygiene factors:** Every call references transcription, summarisation, or AI-driven quality management; now table-stakes not differentiators.

5. **Team adoption and training friction:** Construction, manufacturing, financial services all cite user adoption, training, or skill gaps as implementation risks. Fortay education and CSM involvement critical.

## 8. SURPRISING OR COUNTERINTUITIVE INSIGHTS

- **Finance/wealth company paradox:** 11 months of vendor evaluation (Zoom, NICE, 8x8) but still indecisive; suggests sales process complexity or internal politics blocking decisions despite technical clarity. Risk of indefinite delay.

- **CSM as retention tool more powerful than product:** Lynsey's account base (Garden Court Chambers, Royal College of Paediatricians) holding via trust and relationship, not feature parity. Suggests Fortay's margin and differentiation lives in service, not tech.

- **Construction company bought Zoom phone, rejecting it:** Despite Mark demo and rollout, adoption failed due to poor support and user experience. Indicates implementation and hand-holding matter more than product capability. Suggests Fortay's managed services revenue critical.

- **Small org (Covermore, 300 chats/month) evaluating enterprise platform (Genesys):** Mismatch suggests previous sales team sold wrong tier or implementation bloat. Boost AI as "simpler alternative" signals market demand for no-code, lightweight AI.

- **Screwfix AI savings (£25k/month) not driving fast closure:** Despite quantified ROI, no formal proposal advanced. Suggests CFO approval cycles or internal IT resistance to vendor change override economic case.

- **Manufacturing/PSTN/Mitel mess spawns no urgency:** Call was detailed and factual, yet no follow-up meeting scheduled. Suggests complexity of multi site, multi contract scenarios paralyses decision making even when deadline is imminent (June 2026).

## CONCLUSION: THE "DISAPPEARANCE" PROBLEM

Across 14 calls, several patterns explain why prospects go quiet post-proposal:

1. **Internal politics and multi-stakeholder alignment delay closure** (Finance company, 11 months in; Screwfix no formal proposal)
2. **AI proposition not yet proven in buyer's vertical** — Fortay still building case studies and referenceable customers
3. **Implementation risk > product risk** — buyers fear change management and adoption more than technology choice
4. **Contact centre licensing as price threshold** — tiers (back office £X, contact centre 6X) create sticker shock and budget cycles
5. **Fortay lacks customer success depth in post-sale** — CSM covering renewals but not implementation hand holding for new deals

The AI proposition works at discovery phase but struggles at commitment phase. Likely cause: prospects need proof their vertical (manufacturing, legal, financial services) has deployed similar solutions successfully. Fortay's case study bank insufficient.
